## 1. Direct answers

**1. What are we building.** An artificial universe with a small, explicit constitution, inside which a person in a headset and an AI (Ego) can create things by saying what they are, and in which everything that then happens is a consequence of the laws rather than of a script. The product the user sees is a VR workshop; the thing being built underneath is a state (parts, joints, materials, charges, temperatures, damage, minds) and a law kernel that decides what that state may become. Rendering, sound, Ego's advice and the tablet are observations of that state, not sources of truth about it.

**2. The role of the law hierarchy.** It is the constitution. Five axioms generate a few meta-laws, which constrain what any lower law may say; the lower laws are derived, with their assumptions written down; constitutive data sits beneath them; numerical methods realise them with stated proof obligations; tests check the obligations. Its job is threefold: to make whole classes of invalid states unreachable rather than detected; to give construction a direction (downward: intent to structure to consequence); and to give debugging the same path in reverse (symptom to violated invariant to the realisation that broke its obligation).

**3. The purpose of the mathematical firewall.** To make the set of reachable states closed: every writer to the world (physics tick, your hands, Ego, loading a save, a script) passes through the same admissibility structure, and the structure is such that a forbidden state has no admissible predecessor. Where a method cannot make a violation unreachable (floating point, truncated iteration), the firewall reduces to an exact, cheap check of a sufficient condition at the one exit of the method, with a fallback that is provably safe. The row solver's "ends with g ≤ 0" is the first such exit.

**4. The vocabulary, as I now use it.**

| Term | What it is | Example here |
|---|---|---|
| Patch | A change that stops one observed failure without naming the law it serves; it leaves a gap at its edges | "servo stops at the command's extremes" (R8's servo clause) |
| Rule | A named behaviour held for every build, found after a break, with a test; it may be a corollary or an unexplained constraint | R6, R13 |
| Derived law | A statement proved from laws above it under stated assumptions; it may be kept as a named corollary but adds no new constraint | F-1.3 (centre of mass of a closed system moves uniformly) |
| Fundamental law | The most general operational statement obtainable from the axioms on a structure; domain-independent | F-3 admissible evolution (Gauss/Moreau) |
| Meta-law | A constraint on what a valid lower law may be | passivity (every element is a store, a convex dissipator, a Dirac junction or a declared source) |
| Axiom | An irreducible assumption the universe makes before anything is derived; its removal has a countermodel | A-3 convex dissipation |
| Constitutive law | A relation whose form is constrained by the laws but whose numbers come from measurement | μ of rubber on concrete; a battery's OCV curve |
| Numerical realisation | A discrete method that implements a law, carrying the obligation to preserve its invariants exactly where possible and within a stated bound otherwise | projected Gauss-Seidel with warm start |
| Implementation rule | A specific coding consequence of the above, replaceable without changing what reality means | "linear rows push about one lever point" |

**5. "Write access to simulated reality" in this architecture.** A writer proposes a transformation of the state, in the vocabulary of the document (place this part, join these two, set this material, add this motor, say this to the mind). The kernel accepts it only if the resulting state is admissible, or projects it onto the nearest admissible state (snap to touching, step to the next stocked size), or refuses with the principle violated. Having written state, the writer gets no say over what the state does next: the tick is the only thing that advances time. So writing is to the present, never to the future.

**6. What authority the AI has.** To propose: designs, parts, joints, materials, controllers (minds, gaits, rhythms), places, creatures, communities, and changes to any of them; to choose among admissible alternatives; to explain; to run experiments (a second world on a bench); to propose a replacement implementation for a realisation that broke its obligation.

**7. What authority the AI does not have.** To set a number that is not a measurement, a declared estimate or a derived quantity; to apply a force, impulse or displacement directly; to give a body energy or momentum; to read a quantity no sensor at that position could read; to alter a constitutive datum; to skip, weaken or bypass the admissibility gate; to change an axiom or meta-law; to declare a failure "flaky".

**8. What stays with the kernel.** Admissibility (geometry, constraints, stops, overlap, realisability); the tick (the only writer of the future); the conservation and passivity accounting; the sensors (what a controller may know); the acceptance or refusal of every proposal, from whichever writer.

**9. When the AI proposes something impossible.** The gate answers with the principle the proposal breaks, the nearest admissible alternative where one is cheap to compute (the next stocked size, the touching position, the smaller swing), and nothing is written. The AI may revise and propose again. The world never holds the impossible state, not even for one tick.

**10. Causal realism versus visual realism.** Visual realism asks whether a frame looks right. Causal realism asks whether what happens next is right when nobody anticipated the situation: the pipe that was a strut becomes a lever, a conductor, a projectile, by the same laws, without anyone having written those cases. Visual fidelity is a property of one observation; causal fidelity is a property of the state and the laws, and it is what survives an unplanned interaction.

**11. An authoritative state beneath the rendering.** The document plus the live physical state is the only truth; the renderer, the audio, the tablet, Ego's reports and the save file are derived views. A thing exists, with mass, temperature, charge, damage and history, whether or not it is in view. Two viewers see the same chair because there is one chair.

**12. Scripted behaviour versus emergence.** Objects carry properties (geometry, material, ports, constitutive data, internal state) and controllers carry decision rules; behaviour is what the laws make of those under the circumstances. An object does not carry "what happens when I fall". Where the project still scripts consequences (a walk tuned against false stops; a servo model separate from the motor model it is made of), that is legacy, listed in §7.

**13. Where scripting still belongs.** In controllers (a mind's choices, a gait's rhythm, a PLC's ladder) because those are design decisions a maker really makes; in intent interpretation (words to specification); in tests and experiments; in Ego's advice. A script may decide what to try; it may never decide what results.

**14. "The AI specifies state; physics writes the future."** The AI's output is a proposed present: what exists, where, made of what, wired how, told what. The tick, applying the laws to that present, produces the next present. Nothing the AI writes contains a clock, a future pose, a scheduled event or an outcome.

## 2. How I am operating now: rules, decisions, tests, assumptions, refusals

**Rules I follow.**
1. Nothing works that would not work in real life; a number is a measurement with a source, a declared estimate with its derivation, a derived quantity, or a numerical parameter with its accuracy statement.
2. Every fix leaves a law or an invariant and a test that fails without it; a fix that cannot name its law is a patch and is not merged.
3. One authority per physical interaction: a law has one implementation and many tests, never two implementations.
4. The energy and momentum books must close; a residual is reported as the integrator's, never hidden.
5. A controller sees only what its sensors could sense; it acts only through ports.
6. Hardware limits are geometry; commands live inside them.
7. Thresholds are dimensionless or scale with a stated length, time or mass; Earth's gravity appears nowhere except as the environment's value.
8. Branch and attribution rules as given; a merge only when CI is green.

**Decision process for a change** (what I did today for the solver and the servo, in that order): state the invariant the behaviour must satisfy; find the identity or theorem that makes it hold for all inputs, not for the case at hand; write the property test over random inputs first; implement; measure; run the whole suite; read every regression as either a tolerance that was hiding a violation or a real break of a different invariant; name the law in the commit.

**Tests I run.** Unit (vitest), conformance (physics against worked examples with sources), golden (magnets against closed forms), property-based (the new firewall file: random rows, bounds, targets, warm starts, pass counts), e2e on an emulated Quest (Playwright + iwer), the stress web and immune suites on demand, the tick budget as a ratio to a yardstick, and a profile when a cost claim is made.

**Assumptions I make** are the model assumptions in §3.1, plus: Jolt's own solver is passive and momentum-exact to float32 for what it handles alone (tested, not proved); the desktop container's timings bound the headset's only up to an unmeasured factor.

**What I refuse.** To tune a controller until a test passes; to widen a tolerance to hide a drift; to add an exception list; to add a constant without a kind; to let two solvers own one contact; to let a mind read the world; to call a failure a flake; to merge with a red suite.

**Legacy approaches replaced today or in the committed branch.** Per-row allocation in the solver (replaced by one buffer); separate lever points (replaced by p*, momentum-exact); energy of the re-solve unaccounted (now per row, exact); passivity assumed (now checked at the solver's one exit); the servo's stops at its command (designed out, patch saved); the law list as prose (being replaced by a derivation hierarchy).

**Legacy approaches that remain.** Everything in §7. The most important: the walker's tuned gait; the servo as its own actuator model; the watchdog's fifteen separate checks; the angular damping; four hidden 9.81s; the 1 m/s no-bounce floor; the position closure from a root; minds reading the world; constants without kinds.
## 3. The hierarchy as I now hold it

F1–F6 of the first pass are not fundamental. Four of them derive from two axioms; one splits; one dissolves into three different levels. The result is below, written top down. IDs: A axioms, M model assumptions, ML meta-laws, FS fundamental structures, F fundamental laws (with decimal descendants), C constitutive, R numerical realisations, D domain laws, I implementation consequences, P property tests. A node's parents are listed; a node with more than one parent is a dependency, not just an ancestry.

### 3.1 Model assumptions (the scope, not laws; each has a stated limit)

| ID | Assumption | Kind | What breaks without it | Where it stops holding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M-1 | Classical mechanics in Euclidean 3-space with one global clock; the ground, the sea and the air are the non-simulated boundary with a rest frame | physical | no rest frame for friction and drag; no notion of "falling" | relativistic effects are 10⁻¹⁶ and below double precision: unrepresentable, so not small but absent; no rotating frame (Coriolis 1.5×10⁻⁵ g left out) |
| M-2 | Bodies are rigid; joined parts are rigid clusters; a breakable part is segments on plastic hinges | physical | no inertia tensor; no joints | flex, waves, creep, fatigue are absent; vibration above 45 Hz appears as heat |
| M-3 | Gravity is a uniform field g, an environment parameter | physical | no weight | tidal gradient 3×10⁻⁶ s⁻² left out; g is a vector, not 9.81 |
| M-4 | Solids do not interpenetrate: the admissible set includes gap ≥ 0 | geometric | no contact | the solver's slop (2 mm) is the discrete tolerance of this |
| M-5 | The universe is computed: discrete time (1/90 s), double precision in the re-solve, single precision in Jolt, bounded resources | computational | nothing | every exactness claim is "to rounding" |

### 3.2 Axioms (the minimum law basis)

**B = { A-1, A-2, A-3, A-4, A-5 }**

For each: ID, name, type, parent, statement, assumptions, children, proof status, exactness, where it stops holding.

**A-1 Port structure (Dirac).**
- Type: axiom, physical-structural. Parent: none.
- Statement: the world is a set of elements, each a store with energy function H_k(x_k), a dissipator, a source, or a power-conserving transformer, interconnected only through ports (effort e, flow f, power e·f) by junctions D that are Dirac structures: for every (f, e) ∈ D, Σ e_i f_i = 0. An ideal constraint is such a junction. Every subsystem, including a controller and a sensor, is an element with ports and has no other coupling to the world.
- Assumptions: M-1, M-5 (a state exists and is finite).
- Children: ML-1 exchange antisymmetry; ML-3 passivity (with A-3); ML-4 admissibility (with M-4); ML-7 causal information; FS-1, FS-2, FS-3, FS-5.
- Proof status: axiom (van der Schaft & Jeltsema 2014 for the composition theorem that makes it worth having: interconnection of Dirac structures is Dirac).
- Exactness: exact in the continuous model; the realisation preserves its consequences to rounding (momentum) or by an exit check (passivity).
- Stops holding: anything coupled to the simulation other than through a port (a script setting a pose; a mind reading a position) is outside the axiom and therefore outside the guarantees.
- Independence test: remove A-1 and keep the rest. Symmetry (A-2) alone does not give pairwise equal-and-opposite interaction (a body in an external potential is symmetric under joint translation of body and field but exchanges momentum with nothing simulated); convex dissipation (A-3) says nothing about conservation; A-4, A-5 are about realisation and data. Countermodel: a world in which a controller writes velocities directly satisfies A-2..A-5 and conserves nothing. So A-1 is independent.

**A-2 Symmetry (equivariance).**
- Type: axiom, mathematical-physical. Parent: none.
- Statement: every law commutes with the action of G = E(3) × (time translation) × (unit rescaling): for a Euclidean motion ρ and a law L, L(ρ·s) = ρ·L(s); laws depend on relative configuration only; and every law is dimensionally homogeneous (invariant in form under rescaling of units).
- Assumptions: M-1 (a Euclidean space to act on).
- Children: F-1 conservation of linear and angular momentum and energy (Noether, via ML-1); ML-2 covariance and dimensional homogeneity; F-5 similarity (Buckingham); the frame tests.
- Proof status: axiom; its consequences are theorems (Noether 1918; Buckingham 1914).
- Exactness: exact in the model; the ground frame breaks the symmetry physically (M-1), so momentum exchanged with the ground is external, by design.
- Stops holding: any constant with units that is not a declared datum breaks the scaling part; Earth's g written into a law breaks it (found in four places, §7).
- Independence test: remove A-2. A-1 still gives power conservation and third-law pairs at junctions, but the pair force may depend on absolute position (a "preferred place"), so momentum is not conserved and a creature behaves differently when the scene is rotated; nothing else forbids it. Countermodel: an interaction potential U(x_a + x_b) satisfies A-1 and conserves neither momentum nor the rotated behaviour. Independent.

**A-3 Convex dissipation (the arrow of time).**
- Type: axiom, physical (second law, stated constitutively). Parent: none.
- Statement: every dissipative response is a subgradient of a convex potential Φ(v) with Φ(0) = 0 = min Φ: λ ∈ −∂Φ(v). Hence ⟨λ, v⟩ ≤ 0 (dissipation is nonnegative) and the response is the maximum-dissipation choice within its admissible set (Moreau 1988; Halphen & Nguyen 1975).
- Assumptions: none beyond a velocity to dissipate against.
- Children: ML-3 passivity (with A-1); F-4 maximum dissipation (friction, plastic hinges, eddy currents, drag, impacts); F-2.4 restitution 0 ≤ e ≤ 1; the sign constraints on every constitutive dissipator (μ ≥ 0, R ≥ 0, D ⪰ 0).
- Proof status: axiom.
- Exactness: exact in the continuous model; at the solver's exit it is checked (g ≤ 0), with a measured leak for truncated friction solves.
- Stops holding: non-associated laws (velocity-strengthening friction, negative resistance) are excluded by it; if a domain needs one, that domain is outside A-3 and must say so.
- Independence test: remove A-3. A-1 with a time-reversible interconnection admits a negative resistor (e = −R f) as a perfectly consistent element; energy then grows without a source and nothing in A-1, A-2, A-4, A-5 forbids it. Independent.

**A-4 Realisation fidelity.**
- Type: axiom, computational (about the artificial universe, not about nature). Parent: none.
- Statement: the discrete realisation of a law preserves exactly every invariant that admits an exact discrete analogue (momentum through antisymmetric impulses; passivity through a convex projection or an exit check; constraint satisfaction through projection) and bounds the error of the rest with a stated order; every constant in the realisation is of a declared kind (physical constant, datum, numerical parameter with accuracy, labelled estimate); time scales faster than the step are resolved (substeps) or integrated implicitly and declared damped; cost is bounded and measured.
- Assumptions: M-5.
- Children: ML-5 realisation law; R-1 to R-11; the budget test; the constant-kind test (not yet written).
- Proof status: axiom (it is a policy about methods; its instances are theorems: theorem A, the ΔK identity, Moreau's decomposition).
- Exactness: by definition, mixed; each child states its own.
- Stops holding: it is not a statement about the physics; a method can satisfy it and still realise the wrong law.
- Independence test: remove A-4. The continuous laws stand; a realisation may then integrate with explicit Euler, allocate per row, hide an Earth-gravity fallback and still be "correct physics" on paper. Countermodel: the A14 explicit gyroscopic step added energy every tick while implementing an exact law. Independent of A-1..A-3, A-5.

**A-5 Empirical grounding and realisability.**
- Type: axiom, empirical and domain-specific (the bridge to the real world). Parent: none.
- Statement: every constitutive number is a measurement of the real material or component, with its source, or an estimate labelled with its derivation; every structure that enters the world is one that can be made: from stock and hardware that are sold, by a process that works that material and shape, joined only where surfaces touch and only where the maker of a bought part allows.
- Assumptions: there is a real world to measure.
- Children: ML-6 grounding; R1–R5, R11 (realisability rules); every constitutive law's data; I3e.
- Proof status: axiom.
- Exactness: not a mathematical property; it is checked by sources and by the realisability gate.
- Stops holding: fiction. A fictional material or creature is admitted only with its numbers declared as estimates and its unmakeable parts declared as such (§5 K, L).
- Independence test: remove A-5. A universe with steel ten times as strong, batteries of arbitrary capacity and joints through thin air is internally consistent under A-1..A-4. Independent.

Could any of the five derive from something deeper? A-1 and A-3 are both expressible in one geometric statement (a Dirac structure with resistive ports whose constitutive relation is monotone), which is how port-Hamiltonian theory writes them; but the monotonicity is a separate assumption inside that statement, so merging them would hide an axiom inside a definition. I keep them apart. A-2 and A-1: a Dirac structure invariant under a group gives the conserved momenta as Casimirs; the group is still a separate assumption. A-4 and A-5 are not physics and cannot descend from it.

### 3.3 Meta-laws (what any valid lower law must be)

| ID | Meta-law | Parents | Statement | What it forbids a lower law from being |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ML-1 | Exchange antisymmetry (the cut law) | A-1 | For any cut separating X from Y, the flux of any conserved quantity from X to Y equals minus the flux from Y to X; conservation of the total is the sum of boundary fluxes | a one-sided force; a torque from a force pair not on one line; a work term with no counter-term |
| ML-2 | Covariance and homogeneity | A-2 | A law is written in relative poses and invariants, with every term dimensionally balanced; thresholds are dimensionless or scale with a declared quantity | a bare 9.81; a 1 m/s; an absolute position |
| ML-3 | Passivity by composition | A-1, A-3 | For any subsystem, Ḣ = −Σ⟨∂Φ_k, v_k⟩ + Σ_ports e·f ≤ Σ_ports e·f; interconnecting passive subsystems through Dirac junctions is passive (van der Schaft) | an element that is neither store, convex dissipator, Dirac junction nor declared source |
| ML-4 | Admissibility and closure | A-1 (workless constraints), M-4 | Constraint reactions lie in the normal cone N_A(q) of the admissible set and do no work on admissible velocities; the flow satisfies T(A) ⊆ A | a reaction that pulls (a contact), a stop that commands, a "teleport" |
| ML-5 | Realisation law | A-4 | Exact where an exact discrete analogue exists; bounded elsewhere with the bound stated; faster than the step means substep or implicit-and-declared; one exit, one check | explicit integration of a stiff mode; a parameter with no accuracy statement; a tolerance that hides a drift |
| ML-6 | Grounding and realisability | A-5 | Data with sources; builds closed under making | a typed-in strength; a joint across a gap |
| ML-7 | Causal information | A-1 (scope: controllers), M-1 (time) | A controller is a dynamical element: output(t) = h(state(t), port inputs up to t − latency); its ports are sensors with physical models | a mind reading a pose; a script reading the future |

### 3.4 Fundamental structures (the objects the laws act on)

- FS-1 State: S = D × Q × V × X (defined in §4).
- FS-2 Hamiltonian H = K(V) + U_gravity(Q) + U_spring + U_band + U_magnet + U_chemical(X) + U_thermal(X).
- FS-3 Constraint set: bilateral C(q) = 0 (joints, bonds, latches), unilateral C(q) ≥ 0 (contacts, stops, ropes, the ground); A = {C = 0, C ≥ 0}.
- FS-4 Dissipation potentials Φ_k: Coulomb friction (support function of the cone), drag (quadratic), eddy (½qᵀDq), plastic hinge (support function of |M| ≤ M_p), electrical resistance (½Ri²), impact.
- FS-5 Ports and sources: batteries (finite chemical store), hands (external port), the ground frame (external), sensors (signal ports), controllers (cyber elements).
- FS-6 Symmetry group G = E(3) × R_time × R⁺_scale.

### 3.5 Fundamental laws, with descendants

**F-1 Conservation (A-2 through ML-1 on FS-5).** For each one-parameter subgroup of G with a conserved current: translations give Σp = const, rotations Σ(x × p + Iω) = const, time Σ H = const, for a closed system; for an open one, the change equals the boundary flux.
- F-1.1 Pair interactions are equal and opposite on one line (relative-pose dependence). Exact.
  - F-1.1.1 (realisation obligation, R-1) The discrete impulse pair acts about one point. Exact to rounding. Test P-1.
  - F-1.1.2 (realisation obligation, R-2) A position correction is momentum-free in position space: Σ m_i δx_i = 0 and Σ m_i x_i × δx_i + I_i δθ_i = 0 for internal corrections. **Not yet held by closeMechanism** (§6.4).
- F-1.2 A joint's load is the momentum flux across the cut, the same from either side (I3d, R9). Derived.
- F-1.3 The centre of mass of a system with no horizontal external impulse keeps its horizontal velocity (the frictionless-floor corollary). Derived.
- Stops holding: Jolt's float32 (10⁻⁷ relative per operation); momentum given to the non-simulated ground is external by M-1.

**F-2 Energy balance and passivity (ML-3 on FS-2, FS-4, FS-5).** Ḣ = −Σ_k ⟨∂Φ_k, v_k⟩ + Σ_ports e·f, with each dissipation term ≥ 0 (A-3), each source a finite store debited by exactly its port power, and dissipated power becoming internal energy of the parts (first law).
- F-2.1 Closed, undriven, frictionless: H constant (I3b). **Violated today by R-3/R-2 integration (A5)**: a body orbiting a joint loses cos²(ωdt) of its kinetic energy per tick.
- F-2.2 Sources are finite: a battery gives P = V·I at its terminals, loses I²R inside, and holds Q(t) = Q₀ − ∫I dt at its maker's rate-dependent capacity (R10).
- F-2.3 Element laws (A-1): store, dissipator, transformer (gear: ω_out = ω_in/N, τ_out = N τ_in), gyrator (DC motor: τ = k i, e = k ω), junction. Composition is passive by ML-3.
  - F-2.3.1 A servo is a motor, a gear and a controller: with V = i R + k ω and τ = k i, through N: T_stall = N k V_s / R, ω₀ = V_s / (k N), τ = T_stall(1 − ω/ω₀); its rotor weighs J_m N² at the horn; it draws i = τ/(N k) from its battery and heats by i²R. **Designed; the current servo (R12) is a separate model that lacks all four consequences.**
- F-2.4 Impacts: a restitution 0 ≤ e ≤ 1 with the restitution phase's work equal to −e² times the compression phase's (Stronge's energetic coefficient), so no frictional impact gains energy. **The current Newton restitution is passive only in single contacts.**
- F-2.5 Heat: dissipated power enters the parts on the row (Blok's partition for sliding; Taylor–Quinney for plastic work), conducts and radiates by constitutive law, raises temperature by m c; temperature feeds back into resistance (copper α), magnet remanence and strength (C-layer).
- F-2.6 Discrete passivity (R-1 obligations, A-4):
  - F-2.6.1 Identity: ΔK = Σ_r λ_r (w_r⁻ + w_r⁺)/2, exact for any impulses. Test P-2.
  - F-2.6.2 For any Λ, ΔK − Λᵀt = g(Λ) − ½ΛᵀΓΛ with g(Λ) = ½Λᵀ(A+Γ)Λ + Λᵀ(w⁻ − t); so g(Λ_final) ≤ 0 is sufficient for passivity whatever the solve did. Checked at the exit. Tests P-3, P-4.
  - F-2.6.3 Theorem A: from Λ = 0 with fixed bounds, projected Gauss-Seidel never raises g. Proof: each update is the exact minimiser of the convex g along its coordinate within the box; a block update minimises over the block. Test P-3.
  - F-2.6.4 At convergence every passive row has λ w⁺ ≤ 0 (KKT); the leak Σ max(0, λw⁺) measures truncation. Test P-5.
- Stops holding: finite iteration with friction (measured as leak, not assumed zero); A5 until RATTLE.

**F-3 Admissible evolution (ML-4 on FS-3; Gauss 1829, Moreau 1988).** Among all accelerations consistent with the constraints, the actual one minimises ½(a − a_free)ᵀ M (a − a_free); at velocity level, u⁺ = P_M[T_A(q)](u⁻ + M⁻¹F dt), the projection in the kinetic metric onto the tangent cone of the admissible set, with reactions in the normal cone.
- F-3.1 Bilateral constraints are workless (d'Alembert) and the flow stays on the manifold: constraint residual bounded, drift removed by a momentum-free projection (R6, R13). Derived from A-1.
- F-3.2 Unilateral constraints are complementarity: λ ≥ 0, C ≥ 0, λ C = 0 (contacts, stops, ropes). Derived from A-1 + M-4 (contact can push, not pull).
- F-3.3 Projection onto a closed convex cone containing 0 shrinks the kinetic metric: ‖P u‖_M ≤ ‖u‖_M (Moreau's decomposition). Hence ideal constraints are passive without invoking A-3. Derived.
- F-3.4 Hardware limits are part of A; commands are inputs inside A. A servo's travel is where its gears end; its swing is what it is told. Derived (the correction of R8's servo clause).
- F-3.5 Discrete obligations: residual after a tick O(r(ωdt)²) and removed on the next (R-2); transport resolution v dt ≤ ℓ for anything that may cross a boundary (R-4); the position pass momentum-free (F-1.1.2).
- Stops holding: the velocity rows use tick-start Jacobians while positions move on a straight line (the A5 mechanism); RATTLE (velocity projected onto the tangent at the new position) is the repair.

**F-4 Maximum dissipation (A-3 on FS-4).** λ ∈ −∂Φ(v) ⇔ λ = argmax_{λ' ∈ K} (−λ'·v) when Φ is the support function of a convex set K. Friction (K: |λ_t| ≤ μλ_n), plastic hinge (|M| ≤ M_p), the stuck magnet's wrench set (M5: pressing, within friction, pressure centre inside the footprint, twist within μNr̄), eddy drag (−Dq, D ⪰ 0), air and water drag, inelastic impact. One solver realises all of them: clamping an accumulated impulse to its bounds is the projection onto K. Derived.
- Stops holding: the size of K is data (C-layer); a non-convex K would leave A-3.

**F-5 Covariance and similarity (ML-2).** Laws in intrinsic variables; by the Π theorem, behaviour depends on dimensionless groups: Froude v²/(gL) for gait and waves, Strouhal fA/U for swimming and flapping, Reynolds for drag regimes, magnetic Reynolds μ₀σuℓ for eddy validity, the servo's ω₀·T_loop for its loop, ω dt and v dt/ℓ for the realisation. Derived.
- Stops holding: wherever a dimensional constant was written in (§7 list); those break scale invariance by construction.

**F-6 Causal information (ML-7).** A sensor is an element with a physical model: an encoder reads an angle, a current sense reads i, a load cell reads a contact force, an inertial unit reads a and ω with drift, an eye reads the light field, which travels in straight lines, so a sighting needs a clear ray. A controller's output depends on its state and those readings, with latency. Derived from A-1's scope.
- Stops holding: `mind.ts` today receives your position and the dry-ground predicate from the world directly; that controller is outside F-6 until it is given sensors.

### 3.6 Constitutive layer (C), data with sources

Materials (ρ, E, σ_y, k, c, μ pairs, ferromagnetism, conductivity); fasteners and joints (NDS yield modes, VDI 2230 bolts, weld throat, wood screws by stocked size); springs, ropes, bands (wire data, rope grades, neo-Hookean band); motors (k, R, L, J, gear ratio and efficiency), batteries (OCV(SOC), R_int, rate capacity), wires; magnets (Gilbert model, Br by grade, temperature coefficient); thermal (conductivities, Blok partition, Taylor–Quinney 0.9, Draper point); welding (wires, gases, efficiencies); water (EOS-80), sand (beach-face slope, dune scale); demography (Siler, Coale–Trussell); servo control law and spec (stall, no-load speed, travel, rotor estimate labelled). ## 4. The formal model

- D: the document, a finite graph: parts (kind, dimensions, material, params), connections (kind, frames, params), places, controllers (minds, channels), with identities. Discrete.
- Q: configurations, ∏_i SE(3) over bodies (a part, or a segment of a breakable part), plus joint coordinates implicit in the poses.
- V: velocities, ∏_i se(3), i.e. (v_i, ω_i).
- X: internal state: temperatures per part, charge per battery, winding temperature and current per motor, plastic rotation and damage per bond, latch state per magnetic pair, accumulated impulses per row (warm start, a numerical state), servo aims, each mind's state (urges, goal, memory of sightings), the energy ledger (a bookkeeping state), the random seeds.
- S = D × Q × V × X, with time t and tick count.
- A ⊆ S, the admissible states, is the intersection of:
  1. geometry: every joint's two frames coincide within ε_res and every bond seated; no two solids overlap beyond the slop; every joint within its hardware limits; every rope no longer than its length (within ε);
  2. realisability: every part makeable (stock, process), every joint where surfaces touch, bought parts joined only as allowed;
  3. constitutive range: charge ∈ [0, capacity]; temperature ≥ 0 K (and above, damage is state, not inadmissibility); an intact bond's plastic rotation below its fracture limit (beyond it the bond is broken: a different, admissible state);
  4. finiteness: every number finite; velocities below what one tick can transport (v dt ≤ ℓ, else substepped);
  5. information: every controller state derived only from its sensors' history (a property of X's provenance, not a predicate on X alone).
- T_allowed: (a) the tick, S → S; (b) document commands from the user; (c) document commands from Ego; (d) loading a save; (e) a script or test driving ops. Each is a proposal to the gate; (a) is the only one that advances t.

Intended: T(A) ⊆ A for every T in T_allowed. Where it holds today, where it does not:

| T | Holds? | Where it fails |
|---|---|---|
| tick, geometry | bounded, not exact | residual O(r(ωdt)²), removed next tick; slop; closure not momentum-free |
| tick, energy | no | A5 (angular damping, orbit projection); until the exit check, the re-solve's warm start could add energy; Jolt-side joints untested for passivity |
| tick, momentum | yes in R-1 (to rounding); unproved in R-3 | closure (R-2) at position level |
| tick, information | no | minds read the world |
| user edit | partly | joints snap to touching (R1); realisability for bought parts (R11); overlap on placement not fully refused |
| Ego edit | same gate as the user | same |
| load | yes (I2 byte-exact) | charge not saved (ARCHITECTURE "not yet") |
| script/test | no gate | tests may construct inadmissible states deliberately, by design |

What makes a state inadmissible, my actual classification:
1. Geometric: overlap beyond slop; joint frames apart; a joint past its hardware limit; a rope over-length; a part with no support of its own mass in the build phase is admissible (it will fall) but a part embedded in another is not.
2. Structural-realisability: a joint across a gap; a fastener that reaches nothing; a bought part drilled; a stock size that is not sold; a process that does not work the material.
3. Conservation (a transition property, not a state predicate): a transition whose momentum change has no external impulse, or whose energy rise has no port work; such a transition is refused by construction in R-1 and reported by the ledger elsewhere.
4. Constitutive: a datum outside its source's range (a declared estimate is admitted with its label); charge beyond capacity; a magnet above its Curie point still magnetised.
5. Informational: a controller output that depends on a quantity not among its sensor readings.
6. Numerical: NaN or infinity; a velocity that would cross a feature in one step without substepping; a solve that exits with g > 0.
7. Identity: a document that does not round-trip through the codec; a physics event applied to a document revision it was not computed against (I6, known violation A1).
## 5. Construction under the architecture: twelve things, not built, workflow shown

The same fourteen questions for each. The pattern that repeats: the AI specifies the discrete structure D and the controller designs; the kernel derives everything in Q, V, X and their evolution; data comes from the C-layer; failure is a state transition the laws make; the gate refuses what cannot exist; the property tests are instances of the universal ones (§11) applied to this object; scale enters through F-5.

**A. A simple wooden chair.**
1. Specifies: four legs, a seat, a back, aprons; sections and lengths; the wood; the joints (screwed, glued or doweled) and where.
2. Derives: each part's mass, inertia and centre; the assembly's centre of mass and support polygon; every joint's load under its own weight and under a person (foresight statics: Tarjan bridges, moments summed); fastener capacities (NDS yield modes for screws in fir, end-grain factors); whether it racks (apron stiffness); whether it tips at a lean.
3. Data: wood density, modulus, yield; screw stocked sizes; glue shear strength; friction of wood on floor.
4. Emerges: sagging seat, a leg joint failing when a heavy person sits, sliding when pushed, tipping when leaned back on two legs, creaking (contact audio from the ledger's friction heat).
5. Design decisions: proportions, which joints, where the aprons go. Ego's designer proposes these from the loads with a safety factor; that is a controller choice, in the open.
6. Rejected: a screw that reaches nothing; a seat thinner than any stocked sheet; a joint between legs that do not touch.
7. Tests: supports a stated load without failure; tips at the predicted lean angle; every joint's load equals the branch balance from either side; builds from stock.
8. Scale: a doll's chair is admissible but its screws must be stocked sizes, so it is refused unless re-jointed (glue, pins); strength-to-weight scales as 1/L, so a giant chair of the same proportions fails first at its joints: that falls out of the capacities.
9. Accounting: nothing powered; energy is gravity, friction heat, impact heat; momentum exchanged only with the floor.
10. Sensors: none.
11. Failure: load over capacity at a joint, by F-4 (yield surface) and the fracture state; nothing scripted.
12. Realisability: stock lists, screw sizes, process per material (R2–R4, R11).
13. Laws: F-1.2 (joint loads), F-3 (contacts, joints), F-4 (friction, yield), C (NDS, wood).
14. Generated rather than scripted: everything after the specification.

**B. A high-speed electric motorcycle.**
1. Specifies: frame (sections, material), two wheels (rim, tyre), a motor (a catalogue item or a blueprint with k, R, J, gear ratio), a battery pack (chemistry, cells, series/parallel), a controller with a current limit, wiring, brakes, suspension (springs and dampers), a rider mass, steering geometry.
2. Derives: mass and inertia tensor (rotor J N² added to the wheel's); torque at the wheel from current at speed; top speed where drag equals thrust; acceleration bounded by friction (μ m g) and by wheelie (centre of mass height over wheelbase); range from pack capacity at the drawn current; motor winding temperature over time; brake heat; suspension natural frequencies.
3. Data: motor constants; cell OCV, R_int, rate capacity; tyre μ; drag coefficient and frontal area (declared estimate if no source); spring and damper rates.
4. Emerges: wheel spin at launch, wheelies, fade as the winding heats, range loss at speed, stoppies, instability if the geometry is wrong (wobble is beyond M-2: tyres are rigid here; declared).
5. Design decisions: gear ratio, pack layout, geometry, controller limits.
6. Rejected: torque without current (F-2.3); a wire below the current; a battery of typed-in capacity; a frame member bolted into the motor housing (R11).
7. Tests: acceleration within 3% of Newton from the datasheets (as the kart already is); energy out of the pack equals wheel work plus every loss; stall current at the limit; the ledger closes.
8. Scale: a half-scale bike at the same Froude number corners alike; its motor must be a real smaller motor, not a scaled one.
9. Accounting: pack → copper → shaft → wheel → road (friction) → drag heat; brakes to heat; every joule booked.
10. Sensors: wheel speed (encoder), current, winding temperature; the rider's own senses are out of scope.
11. Failure: burnout by winding temperature; a joint failing under a landing; tyre slip beyond μ.
12. Realisability: catalogue motors and cells; frame from stocked tube; welds rated by throat.
13. Laws: F-2.2, F-2.3, F-2.5, F-4, F-1, C (motor, battery, tyre).
14. Generated: all dynamics; scripted: only the controller's current limit and the rider's commands.

**C. A four-legged robotic animal.**
1. Specifies: body plate; four legs of thigh, shank, foot (dimensions, materials); eight servos by their spec (stall torque, no-load speed, travel, rotor estimate); a battery; and a controller: a per-leg oscillator with load feedback (the limb ganglion), plus a mind with sensors (an eye with field and range, a load cell per foot, encoders).
2. Derives: every link's mass and inertia; what each servo really turns (R7's inertia, now automatic because the motor acts on the true system); current per servo at its torque and speed; battery life; ground reaction forces; friction limits on each foot; whether it stands (support polygon versus centre of mass); the gait, from the oscillators coupled through the ground (Owaki's Tegotae: each leg's phase advances faster when unloaded).
3. Data: servo spec (sourced), rotor inertia (estimate, labelled), foot rubber μ, battery cells, link material.
4. Emerges: walk at low Froude number, trot at higher, stumbling on a slope, falling when shoved beyond the support polygon, getting up if the legs can reach, a limp when a leg is lost (the other legs' feedback re-times), slowing as the battery sags.
5. Design decisions: the controller's gains and the sensor set; leg proportions.
6. Rejected: a servo commanded past its travel (clamped to A); a leg lighter than its material allows; sight through walls.
7. Tests: walks at any gravity and leg length that give the same Froude number; frictionless floor gives zero net motion (F-1.3); the ledger closes with servo work booked; standing with a mass on top until the support polygon is crossed.
8. Scale: at ten times the size, the same Froude number means √10 the speed and the frequency over √10; the controller written in Froude terms needs no retuning; the servos must be real larger servos.
9. Accounting: battery → servo copper → rotor kinetic → link motion → friction and impact heat.
10. Sensors: encoders (angle), current sense (torque), foot load cells, an eye with line of sight; the mind gets only those.
11. Failure: a stalled servo heating; a shank snapping on a fall (fracture state); the battery flat.
12. Realisability: micro servos as bought parts (R11), printed links, stocked screws.
13. Laws: F-2.3.1, F-3.4, F-4, F-5 (Froude), F-6, F-1.3.
14. Generated: the gait itself; scripted: the oscillator law and the mind's urges.

**D. A suspension bridge.**
1. Specifies: towers, deck sections, main cables, hangers, anchorages; materials and sections; span.
2. Derives: the cable's catenary shape under the deck's weight; hanger tensions; tower compression and buckling margin; deck bending between hangers; natural frequencies of the deck (within M-2's rigid-segment limit: a segmented deck on plastic hinges gives a discrete mode set, which is a declared approximation).
3. Data: steel strengths, rope grades (k, E, density), concrete, soil bearing (estimate).
4. Emerges: sag with load, sway under wind loads (if a wind field is given), oscillation under moving traffic, a hanger snapping and the deck twisting, collapse if the towers buckle.
5. Design decisions: tower height, cable sag ratio, hanger spacing.
6. Rejected: a massless cable; a cable that pushes (ropes are tension-only by F-3.2); an anchorage in nothing.
7. Tests: statics match the catenary solution; every hanger's tension equals the branch balance; wind load energy balance; momentum of the whole bridge-plus-traffic conserved horizontally except through the ground.
8. Scale: a model bridge at 1:100 has the same stress at 1/100 the load only if materials scale, which they do not; the scale model therefore over-performs in strength and under-performs in deflection similarity; the laws show both.
9. Accounting: gravity, wind work, traffic kinetic, damping heat in the hinges.
10. Sensors: none unless instrumented.
11. Failure: yield, buckling (Euler/Johnson in the ganglia), hanger fracture, resonance beyond damping (within the segment model).
12. Realisability: stocked rope and sections; welds; a span beyond any stocked rope is refused.
13. Laws: F-1.2, F-3, F-4, C (ropes, steel, buckling).
14. Generated: all statics and dynamics.

**E. A human-scale house.**
1. Specifies: footprint, floors, walls, roof, openings; framing sections and spacing; sheathing; materials; connections (nails, screws, hangers); utilities as subsystems (§F).
2. Derives: dead load per member; load paths to the ground; member stresses and deflections; roof load by snow (an environment input); racking under wind.
3. Data: lumber grades, nail and screw capacities (NDS), sheathing, concrete.
4. Emerges: sag, a joist failing under a piano, a roof lifting in wind if hold-downs are weak, a wall racking without sheathing.
5. Design decisions: layout, spacing, where the shear walls are.
6. Rejected: a beam longer than stocked lumber without a splice; a nail through 300 mm of bracket; a span over the joist's capacity is admitted (it will sag or fail) but flagged by foresight.
7. Tests: every member's load from both sides; the building's weight equals the sum of foundation reactions; realisability of every piece.
8. Scale: a doll's house from real lumber is refused by stock sizes; a giant's house fails at its joints by 1/L.
9. Accounting: static; thermal if heating is modelled (C-layer conduction exists).
10. Sensors: none.
11. Failure: load over capacity; fire (§9.3) by combustion data not yet modelled.
12. Realisability: stocked lumber lengths, sheet sizes, fastener sizes.
13. Laws: F-1.2, F-3, F-4, C (wood, fasteners).
14. Generated: structure; scripted: nothing.

**F. A factory: conveyors, motors, sensors, PLCs, electrical distribution, compressed air.**
1. Specifies: machines as blocks (ganglia archetypes: actuator, reducer, support, structure, control), their ports wired; a distribution: supply, breakers, wires; PLC programs as controllers with sensor and actuator ports; an air system: compressor (motor + pump), receiver (a store), lines (dissipators), cylinders (transformers).
2. Derives: every motor's current and heat; wire loads; breaker trips; conveyor belt tension and slip; cylinder force from receiver pressure; receiver pressure over time from compressor flow minus consumption; what the PLC can see and do.
3. Data: motors, breakers, wire ratings, belt μ, compressor map, line losses.
4. Emerges: a breaker tripping when two machines start together; a receiver running down under heavy demand; a belt slipping under load; a PLC acting late because its scan time is its latency.
5. Design decisions: the ladder logic, the layout, the sizes.
6. Rejected: a PLC output that depends on a quantity with no sensor; a cylinder giving more force than pressure times area; air from nowhere.
7. Tests: electrical power balance (supply = loads + losses); air mass balance; passivity of the whole plant; PLC causality (outputs depend only on sensor history).
8. Scale: the electrical and pneumatic parts are not Froude-scaled; they are specified components, so scale is a choice of components.
9. Accounting: supply → copper → shafts → belts → friction; compressor → receiver (stored pV work) → cylinders → work and losses.
10. Sensors: proximity switches, encoders, pressure transducers, current sense; the PLC gets only those, with scan latency.
11. Failure: overload, overheat, trip, slip, pressure loss; a bearing lost (§9.6).
12. Realisability: catalogue components; stock structure.
13. Laws: F-2.2, F-2.3 (transformer, gyrator), F-4, F-6, plus a pneumatic store law (new C-layer: ideal gas pV = nRT with an isothermal or adiabatic choice declared).
14. Generated: all plant dynamics; scripted: the PLC programs, which are the thing a maker really writes.

**G. A sailboat in waves and wind.**
1. Specifies: hull (shape, mass distribution, ballast), keel, rudder, mast, sail (area, a lift/drag polar as data), rigging (ropes); the sea state and wind field as environment inputs.
2. Derives: buoyancy and righting moment from the immersed shape (face-area water model exists for swimmers; a hull needs the same at larger scale); sail force from the polar at the apparent wind; keel lift; drag; heel angle; heave and pitch in waves.

4. Emerges: heeling, capsize past the vanishing stability angle, broaching, surfing on a wave, a sheet parting under gust load, the boat stalling head to wind.
5. Design decisions: hull form, ballast ratio, rig.
6. Rejected: a sail that gives thrust dead upwind; a keel with no mass; a rope that pushes.
7. Tests: righting moment curve against hydrostatics; sail force passive (it can only take energy from the wind field, a declared external source); momentum exchanged with water and wind only.
8. Scale: Froude similarity governs waves and hull speed (hull speed ∝ √L); a model boat at 1:10 sails in waves of 1:10 length at 1:√10 speed.

12. Realisability: rope grades, sheet sizes, a mast from stocked tube.
13. Laws: F-1, F-2 (external ports), F-3 (ropes one-sided), F-4 (drag), F-5 (Froude), C (buoyancy, polar).

**H. A submarine.**
1. Specifies: pressure hull (shape, material, thickness), ballast tanks, a propulsion motor and battery, control surfaces, a depth sensor and controller.
2. Derives: buoyancy versus mass (neutral when equal), stability (centre of buoyancy above centre of mass), hull stress at depth (σ = p r / t) against yield, crush depth, thrust at speed, battery life, trim response to the planes.

5. Design decisions: tank volumes, planes, the controller.
6. Rejected: a hull thinner than its depth needs is admitted but implodes; a motor without current is refused; a ballast change with no pump work is refused (water moved against pressure costs work).
7. Tests: buoyancy equals displaced weight; depth control causality; energy of pumping booked.
8. Scale: pressure is per depth, not per size; a small hull is stronger (r/t); flow regime by Reynolds, declared.
9. Accounting: battery → motor → thrust → drag heat; pump work → potential energy of displaced water.
10. Sensors: pressure (depth), inertial, speed log.
11. Failure: crush, flooding (a volume gaining water mass over time), battery flat.
12. Realisability: a pressure hull from stocked plate, rolled and welded; welds rated.
13. Laws: F-2.2, F-3, F-4, C (hoop stress, EOS-80), F-6.
14. Generated: all dynamics.

**I. A spacecraft in orbit.**
1. Specifies: a body with mass distribution, tanks (propellant mass as a store), thrusters (mass flow rate and exhaust velocity as spec), reaction wheels (rotor J, motor), solar panels (power source with a stated irradiance), a star tracker and gyros, an attitude controller; and the environment: gravity as a central field (a change of M-3 from uniform to 1/r², a declared environment model) and no ground.
2. Derives: orbit from gravity and velocity; attitude from torques; Δv from the rocket equation (momentum exchange with expelled mass, F-1 with the propellant counted); reaction wheel saturation; power budget.
3. Data: Isp, tank masses, wheel J, panel power, the gravitational parameter.
4. Emerges: orbital decay from no cause (none: no drag in vacuum), tumbling when a wheel saturates, a thruster misfire turning the craft, the centre of mass shifting as propellant leaves.
5. Design decisions: the controller, thruster placement.
6. Rejected: thrust without expelled mass (F-1: momentum must go somewhere simulated or declared external; the exhaust is a declared external sink with its momentum booked); a wheel torque without a motor current.
7. Tests: total momentum of craft plus expelled mass conserved; angular momentum conserved when only wheels act (the craft turns, the wheels counter-turn); energy of the orbit conserved to the integrator's order.
8. Scale: no gravity scaling issue; the magnet cutoff's 9.81 fallback would have been wrong here, which is why it is on the list.
9. Accounting: chemical (propellant) → kinetic (exhaust and craft); electrical (panels) → wheels; no dissipation but wheel bearings.
10. Sensors: gyros with drift (an explicit sensor model), star tracker with field of view.
11. Failure: wheel saturation, propellant exhaustion, a tank rupture.
12. Realisability: components as specified; the frame from stock.
13. Laws: F-1 (with a momentum port for exhaust), F-2.2, F-2.3, F-6; M-3 replaced by a central field.
14. Generated: orbit and attitude; scripted: the controller.

**J. A living forest ecosystem.**
1. Specifies: terrain (a place), species as block archetypes: a tree is a structure (trunk as a beam with growth as a slow change of section and mass), a store of water and carbohydrate with rates; animals as creatures (§C) with minds and sensors and energy budgets; weather as inputs; the genesis blocks for populations (life tables, fertility).
2. Derives: a tree's wind load and the moment at its root; whether it breaks or uproots (soil shear, estimate); an animal's energy cost of walking (servo work, or metabolic rate as data for a biological muscle model, which does not exist yet) against what it eats; population change from births and deaths given the rates.
3. Data: wood properties, growth rates, metabolic rates, species life tables; most are estimates and must be labelled.
4. Emerges: windthrow of the tallest trees, a slope failing when roots are gone, animals congregating where food is, starvation when the budget is negative, populations oscillating.
5. Design decisions: which species, which rates, the minds.
6. Rejected: an animal that moves without energy; a tree that stands on nothing; growth from no store.
7. Tests: energy budget per creature closes; populations follow the life table in the absence of events; momentum and energy of the physical layer as always.
8. Scale: Froude for animals; the elastic similarity of trees (height ∝ d^(2/3), McMahon) if growth is modelled as data.
9. Accounting: sun → stores (declared source) → growth and motion → heat; no free energy.
10. Sensors: sight with line of sight, hearing (not modelled), smell (not modelled).
11. Failure: structural (trees), energetic (animals), demographic (populations).
12. Realisability: not applicable to living things; the place must be a terrain the heightfield can hold.
13. Laws: F-1 to F-6 for the physical layer; the genesis layer is a separate stochastic process layer with its own data and no physics claim.
14. Generated: physical events; the ecosystem's rates are data and are the weakest link (empirically unsupported until sourced).

**K. A fictional creature with unfamiliar anatomy.**
1. Specifies: its body plan as blocks: segments, joints with travel and actuators with stall torque and speed (declared estimates, since no datasheet exists), sensors with fields, a mind; its materials by analogy to real ones (chitin as a composite with sourced numbers, declared as the analogy).
2. Derives: whether it can stand, how it must move to move at all (the limb ganglion finds a gait or fails to), its energy cost, its stability.
3. Data: all estimates, every one labelled; the material analogies sourced.
4. Emerges: a gait nobody designed; a fall when the body plan cannot balance; a limb that cannot lift the body because the actuator estimate is too weak.
5. Design decisions: the plan and the estimates.
6. Rejected: an actuator with no energy source; a material with no analogy; sight without an eye.
7. Tests: the same as §C; plus the label test: every number in the creature carries "estimate".
8. Scale: Froude.
9. Accounting: a declared store (food) → actuators → heat.
10. Sensors: as specified, with physical models.
11. Failure: as §C.
12. Realisability: not makeable by R11; admitted only as a creature, with its parts flagged "not makeable", which is a different admissibility class (living or fictional things are not built, they are placed, and they cannot be joined to builds as if bought).
13. Laws: all physical laws; A-5 satisfied by labelling, not by sources.
14. Generated: everything but the plan.

**L. A completely fictional machine with no real-world equivalent.**
1. Specifies: its function as flows (ways.ts: what it takes and gives), its blocks, its ports; where no real block exists, a declared fictional element with stated store, dissipation and ports.
2. Derives: whether the flow chain is passive and closed (ML-3): a machine that outputs more than it takes is refused at the concept level; what each block carries; whether the real blocks around the fictional one can take its loads.
3. Data: real blocks sourced; the fictional one estimated and labelled.
4. Emerges: its behaviour in the world once placed.
5. Design decisions: the fictional element's constitutive form, which must still be a store, convex dissipator, transformer, gyrator or declared source (A-1, A-3): fiction is allowed in the numbers, not in the structure.
6. Rejected: a free-energy block; a block with a port that is not effort × flow; a non-convex dissipator.
7. Tests: passivity of the machine as a whole; power balance at every junction; the label test.
8. Scale: by its dimensionless groups, once written in them.
9. Accounting: as any machine.
10. Sensors: as specified.
11. Failure: as any machine.
12. Realisability: flagged not makeable where it is not; it can be placed as a fiction, not built.
13. Laws: ML-3 at the concept level (the frontier challenge engine already does this for inventions), then all.
14. Generated: everything but the invention.

What generalises across the twelve: the AI never specifies a consequence; the gate never needs a rule written for the object; the tests are the same tests instantiated; the data is the only place where the real world enters, and where it is missing the label says so.
## 6. Debugging as the reverse traversal

I do not start in the code. I start from the quantity that changed, ask which invariant forbids that change, follow the invariant to its law and the law to the realisations that carry its obligations, and only then open a file. The per-row work, the exit value g, the leak, `warmKept`, the per-joint loads and the ledger's `numerical` term are the instruments; each one is attached to a node in §3.

**"The machine suddenly launched across the room."**

1. Symptom: a large change of the machine's momentum and kinetic energy within a few ticks.
2. Invariants suspected, in order: F-1 (momentum: where did the impulse come from?), then F-2 (energy: was it paid for?). Both must be answered; a launch violates at least one unless an external port supplied both.
3. Governing law: ML-1 and ML-3.
4. Dependency path: F-1 → F-1.1.1 (R-1), F-1.1.2 (R-2), R-3 (Jolt), R-6 (latch release impulse), the external ports (hands, fields: magnets M3, fluids).
5. Instruments: the ledger's `work` by source for those ticks (hands? batteries? magnets?); the row works and `leak` per solve; `warmKept` (did a solve fall back?); the loads on every joint of the machine; `numerical`.
6. Branches:
   - work.hands nonzero: a hand did it (a kinematic grab can supply unbounded energy: ML-3's external port); legitimate.
   - work.magnets or a latch release: M3/M6 path; check the released wrench ΔW against M5's admissible set.
   - all ports zero and `dK` of a solve large with `leak` large: a truncated friction solve (F-2.6.4) or a warm start that ended above zero and was not caught (would be a bug in the exit check: obligation F-2.6.2).
   - all ports zero and `numerical` large: Jolt's step (R-3) or the position closure (R-2): obligation F-1.1.2; the A13 class (float32 inertia) and A14 class (explicit gyroscopic) live here.
   - a joint load spike at one tick with `numerical` small: F-3.5 transport (a fast part crossed a feature; v dt > ℓ) or a stop passed (R-4).
7. Correction is chosen at the node that failed its obligation, never at the symptom: e.g. make the closure mass-weighted (F-1.1.2), not "clamp velocities".
8. Test that prevents recurrence: the universal one for that node, instantiated: random mechanisms, closure leaves Σ m x unchanged; or random rows, exit g ≤ 0.

**6.1 A joint separates.**
Symptom: constraint residual beyond ε_res. → Invariant: F-3.1 (residual bounded). → Law: ML-4. → Path: R-1 (row solve converged? `passes` at cap, residual velocity), R-2 (position pass took out 20%; closure applied to this island?), R-4 (v dt ≤ ℓ for the parts), the MASS_RATIO routing (was the joint even in the re-solve, or left to Jolt's PGS which does not converge heavy-on-light?). → Obligation failed: typically "every joint that carries a load through a light part is re-solved on true inertia" (the routing) or "closure reaches every island". → Correction: route by a convergence criterion rather than a 10:1 ratio; closure by mass-weighted projection. → Test: random chains with mass ratios up to 10⁴, residual < ε after N ticks.

**6.2 A motor produces impossible torque.**
Symptom: τ > k i_limit, or torque with i = 0. → Invariant: F-2.3 (gyrator: τ = k i) and F-2.2 (i from the pack). → Path: electric.ts solve (circuit), driveJoints (the torque Jolt was allowed), bookMotors (what it really applied), settleDrives. → Obligation: the torque limit handed to Jolt equals k i at the solved current; the booked torque is read back from Jolt's λ. → Correction: whichever of the two disagrees with the circuit solve. → Test: for random packs and motors, applied torque ≤ k i(V, ω) every tick; stall current at the limit; the ledger's batteries term equals winding + shaft work.

**6.3 A body gains kinetic energy while nothing powered it.**
Symptom: ΔK > 0 with all ports zero. → Invariant: ML-3. → Path: per-row works (which row did positive work with target 0?), `leak`, `warmKept`, latch releases, the gyroscopic step, Jolt's restitution (F-2.4 with several frictional contacts is a known gap), numerical angular damping (negative, so not this). → Obligation: g ≤ 0 at the exit (R-1); passive restitution (F-2.4). → Correction: at the node. → Test: random passive scenes, K non-increasing per tick to rounding; impact tests with energetic restitution.

**6.4 An object tunnels through a wall.** (And the frictionless walker's 30 cm.)
Symptom: a state outside A (overlap beyond slop, or a wall crossed). → Invariant: ML-4 (T(A) ⊆ A) via F-3.5 (transport). → Path: R-4 (was v dt ≤ ℓ enforced for this body? the substep law covers joints near stops and springs, not every fast body; Jolt's CCD covers bodies it steps), R-2 (a position correction can move a body through a wall if it is large: MAX_CORRECTION 0.2 m is a dimensional cap here). → Obligation: no position move larger than the clearance along its path. → Correction: the position pass checks its path against contacts (a swept move), or limits each correction to the free distance. → Test: random thin walls and fast bodies: never on the far side without a recorded contact.
The frictionless walker: symptom, horizontal centre-of-mass motion with no horizontal external impulse. → F-1.3. → Path: R-1 is now proved exact; R-3's contacts have vertical normals and zero friction; so the suspect is R-2's closure, which moves non-root assemblies whole (position change without the mass weighting that keeps Σ m δx = 0). → Obligation F-1.1.2. → Correction: mass-weighted closure (SHAKE-like projection). → Test: P-6 (closure preserves centre of mass and the angular-momentum position analogue on random mechanisms); the walker test's limit drops from 0.3 m to rounding.

**6.5 A creature sees through an opaque wall.**
Symptom: a mind acts on a position it has no clear ray to. → Invariant: ML-7. → Law: F-6. → Path: mind.ts `sees` (arc and range only), herd.ts (passes `you` from the world). → Obligation: a sighting requires a ray from eye to target unobstructed by any body. → Correction: the sensor model casts the ray; the mind receives sightings, not positions. → Test: a wall between creature and target: no sighting; remove the wall: sighting; the mind's output is identical for identical sighting histories (a determinism-of-information test).

**6.6 A pendulum slowly loses energy.**
Symptom: H decreasing in a closed, frictionless system. → Invariant: F-2.1. → Path: R-3's velocity projection after the straight-line position step (energy loss cos²(ωdt) per tick) and the angular damping 0.02/s (declared numerical). → Obligation: a symplectic constrained step (bounded energy error, no drift). → Correction: RATTLE-type velocity projection onto the tangent at the new position, in the re-solve and for Jolt-side joints; remove the damping. → Test: a pendulum over 60 s keeps its amplitude to the integrator's order; a tumbling rod matches Euler's equations.

**6.7 A tiny creature behaves differently from its scaled-up version when similarity says it should not.**
Symptom: at equal Froude (and Strouhal) numbers, different gait or failure. → Invariant: F-5 via ML-2. → Path: any dimensional constant in the creature's controller or in the laws it meets: rhythm in Hz (should be √(g/L)·Fr-based), stride in metres, NEAR_YOU 1.0 m and ARRIVE 0.3 m in the mind, SLOP 2 mm, MIN_IMPACT 1 m/s, STOP_TURN. → Obligation: thresholds dimensionless or scaled by a declared length. → Correction: rewrite each in terms of body length, √(L/g) and the feature size. → Test: the same creature at 0.1× and 10× size, at the gravity that keeps Fr equal, follows the same dimensionless trajectory within tolerance.

**6.8 A robot behaves differently when the environment is rotated.**
Symptom: equivariance broken. → Invariant: A-2 via ML-2. → Path: anything written in absolute axes: the walker's heading convention (facing +x), the gait's left/right from the body frame (fine), the terrain functions (fine if evaluated in world), magnet pole axis conventions (local, fine), the tangents(n) choice for friction rows (a fixed world axis used to build tangents makes friction history frame-dependent: a candidate), the row grouping keys, the warm-start keys (fine), gravity direction (`floorY`, "up" assumptions in the watchdog: `fell`). → Obligation: every law in relative/intrinsic variables. → Correction: at the node found. → Test: the whole scene rotated about gravity by a random angle: trajectories agree after rotating back; and for a rotated gravity vector, likewise.

**6.9 Two individually valid subsystems create instability when connected.**
Symptom: oscillation or divergence at the connection. → Invariant: ML-3 (passivity composes) and ML-5 (time scales). → Path: is each subsystem actually passive (A-3) or does one carry a source whose port is not declared (a servo loop is a source: its controller gain can make the pair active; two servos fighting through a stiff link is a known active loop)? Is the connection stiff beyond the step (ω dt > c) and integrated explicitly? Is the latch (R-6) alternating with the continuous model? → Obligation: declared sources have bounded power; stiff modes substepped or implicit. → Correction: at the parent: if the composition theorem's hypothesis (passivity) is violated by a controller, the controller is the source and needs its power bound (it already has: stall torque × speed); if the hypothesis holds and it still diverges, the realisation broke ML-5. → Test: two random passive subsystems joined by a random Dirac junction: energy non-increasing; with a servo: energy rise ≤ its booked work.

**6.10 Saving and loading changes the result.**
Symptom: determinism (I5) or identity (I2) broken. → Invariant: A-4's policy: the realisation is a function of the saved state. → Path: state not in the save (battery charge, the warm-start map, minds' seeds, latch states, accumulated plastic rotation, temperatures), floating-point order (Map iteration order after reload), Jolt's internal state (island order, contact cache) not serialisable. → Obligation: S is exactly what is saved, or what is not saved is declared and its effect bounded. → Correction: save every member of X that affects the future; declare Jolt's cache as not saved and re-warm. → Test: save at tick n, load, step k ticks; compare with the unsaved run to a stated tolerance; byte-exact round trip of D.

The symmetry with construction: building walks D → laws → state → behaviour; debugging walks behaviour → state → invariant → law → realisation → obligation → test. The same node list serves both.

### 7.5 Watchdogs: role and classification

A watchdog never enforces reality. It reads instruments and reports that an implementation seems to have failed an obligation of a law, naming the law. Enforcement is the law's realisation (the solver's exit check, the gate). Classification of the fifteen kinds:

| Kind | Class | Note |
|---|---|---|
| nonfinite | DERIVED ASSERTION (A-4 finiteness) | keep |
| drift | DERIVED ASSERTION (F-3.1) | rewrite with a stated bound |
| fell | DIAGNOSTIC | "below the floor" is a symptom of tunnelling (ML-4) |
| tunnel | DERIVED ASSERTION (F-3.5) | keep |
| flung | REDUNDANT with energy/power balance | merge |

## 8. Before writing code for a new domain

**"Add hydraulic systems."** The sequence I follow, before any file is opened:

1. Does the existing structure cover it? A hydraulic circuit is pressure (effort) × volume flow (flow): the same port algebra as electricity and mechanics. A pump is a transformer from shaft (τ, ω) to (p, Q) with displacement D: p D = τ, Q = D ω. A cylinder is a transformer from (p, Q) to (F, v) with area A. An accumulator is a store (gas spring, U = ∫p dV). A valve or line is a dissipator (Φ convex: laminar ½RQ², turbulent ∝ |Q|³). A relief valve is a one-sided constraint (F-3.2) on pressure. So: no new fundamental law; no new meta-law.
2. New constitutive relations: fluid bulk modulus (compressibility as a store), line resistance by diameter and viscosity (Hagen–Poiseuille, laminar; Darcy–Weisbach beyond), pump and valve data, accumulator gas law, seal friction (Coulomb, F-4).
3. New fundamental law? No. New state: pressure per node (a store state), accumulator volume, oil temperature (dissipation heats the oil: F-2.5).
4. Representable with existing elements: yes (store, dissipator, transformer, constraint, source = the pump's motor).
5. Invariants: volume conservation at junctions (ML-1 for mass flow), power conservation at every transformer, passivity of every line and valve, pressure ≥ vapour pressure (cavitation as a state limit).
6. Assumptions: incompressible except the declared bulk modulus; isothermal lines; no fluid inertia below a stated Reynolds number (declared).
7. Numerical: the bulk modulus makes the circuit stiff (ω = √(β A²/(V m))): ML-5 says substep or implicit; the row solver can take a cylinder as a soft row with γ from β, exactly as the servo's soft row.
8. Tests: power balance across every pump and cylinder to rounding; pressure never above relief; the ledger closes with hydraulic heat; a cylinder lifting a mass does m g h of work out of the pump's shaft work plus losses; a scale test (same dimensionless pressure drop at equal Reynolds).

**Briefly, by the same questions:**
- Pneumatics: as hydraulics with a compressible store (ideal gas; isothermal or adiabatic declared), choked flow as a constitutive limit; no new law.
- Combustion: a chemical store (fuel) → heat with a rate law (Arrhenius or a simpler mixing-limited rate, declared) → gas expansion (store) → work; needs a species/mass state and a reaction-rate constitutive model; new state, new C-layer, no new law; A-3 holds (reactions dissipate free energy).
- Fluid flow: beyond face-area drag, a real field needs a new structure (a grid state) with its own realisation obligations (mass and momentum conservation on the grid, CFL = ML-5's transport law); the laws are the same, the FS-1 state grows.
- Heat transfer: exists (conduction, radiation, Blok, Draper); extend to convection as a constitutive film coefficient; no new law.
- Flexible materials: changes M-2 (rigid bodies): new state (strain), new store (elastic energy), new realisation (finite elements or beam elements) with ML-5 obligations; the laws unchanged; the admissible set grows (strain limits).
- Fracture: exists as the plastic-hinge and bond model (F-4 yield surface + history state); extend with fatigue as an accumulated damage state (Miner's rule, C-layer); no new law.
- Batteries: exists (R10); extend with temperature dependence and degradation (a slow state); no new law.
- Electronics: the circuit solver exists for resistive networks; capacitors and inductors are stores, diodes and transistors are constitutive nonlinearities (convex for passive ones, A-3), switches are constraints; timing (clocks) is a controller matter under ML-7; no new law, but numerical stiffness (ML-5).
- Radio communication: a signal port with a channel model (path loss, latency, bandwidth as constitutive data); under ML-7 a radio is a sensor-actuator pair; it adds no physics beyond a tiny power budget; no new law.
- Optics: already implicit in "sight by straight rays"; lenses and mirrors are constitutive (refraction, reflection as laws of geometric optics, which are themselves derivable from Fermat's principle: a variational law of the same family as A-1); no new fundamental law unless diffraction is wanted.
- Biological muscle: an actuator element (Hill model: force-velocity and force-length as constitutive data, a chemical store for its fuel, convex dissipation in its damping) under F-2.3; no new law; the data is where the work is.
- Neural controllers: controllers under ML-7, with learning as a change of the controller's state over time; they add no physics; the gate is unchanged: a neural controller can want anything and get only what its actuators' ports allow.

In none of the thirteen is a new axiom or meta-law needed. Two change a model assumption (fluids, flexible materials), several add state and constitutive data, all add tests. That is the test of the method: new domains add data and realisations, not laws.
## 9. An AI-generated environment: the coastal industrial city in a hurricane

Not a scene generator: a writer of an authoritative state, after which the tick decides everything.

1. Intent → interpretation: words to a specification: a place (coast, river mouth, elevation profile), a settlement block (industrial port), an era, a weather event with its parameters (central pressure, wind field, rainfall rate, surge height), a population block.
2. Semantic plan: a graph of districts, utilities (grid, water, drainage, gas), transport, machines, inhabitants; every node a block with ports.
3. Geography and terrain: generated as a heightfield from the place blocks (beach slope, dune scale, river profile), with ground materials; written to D.
4. Structures: generated from building blocks (frames, cladding, roofs, foundations) with sections, materials and fasteners chosen by the ganglia's workflows for the local loads; each must pass realisability.
5. Materials: all from the C-layer; where a fictional alloy is wanted, it is an estimate, labelled.
6. Utilities: the grid as a circuit (sources, breakers, wires, loads); water and drainage as a hydraulic network with capacities; gas as a pressurised store with lines.
7. Weather: generated as inputs to external ports: a wind field (a time-varying vector field over the terrain), rainfall (mass flux onto surfaces), surge (a rising water level); these are declared external sources, the hurricane's energy being outside the simulated system, as the ground's is.
8. Water: emerges: rain runs off by terrain slope, enters drains, exceeds capacity, floods streets by level; buoyancy and drag act on anything the water reaches (the existing face-area water model); friction of wet surfaces changes by data.
9. Wind: emerges as loads on every structure by its face area and drag coefficient (data); roofs lift when the load exceeds the fastener capacity (F-4, NDS); loose sheets become bodies in the wind.
10. Vehicles and machines: generated from blocks; their behaviour emerges (a car in water floats when buoyancy exceeds weight, loses traction when its wheels do).
11. Electrical: emerges: water reaching a conductor is a resistive path (data: water conductivity) → current → breaker trip or heating; a downed line is a conductor in contact with ground.
12. People and creatures: generated from genesis and creature blocks: bodies, minds with sensors; they see what their eyes have a clear ray to, through rain (a visibility model in the C-layer, if wanted).
13. Sensors and information: everything a controller does comes through its sensors; a pump station's PLC sees its level sensor and nothing else.
14. Damage, energy, failure: all emerge; the ledger books the hurricane's work through its ports; every failure is a load over a capacity or a store running out.
15. Rendering and sound: observations of the state (wind sound from the drag power; rain from impacts).
16. Persistence, save and load: D and X saved; the weather's inputs saved as a seed and parameters so the run is reproducible (I5).
17. Debugging: the instruments of §6 over the whole city: which building's roof left first and under which load; which breaker tripped and on what current.

Generated directly: D (what exists), the external inputs (the storm), the controllers. Emergent: everything that happens.

**The fifteen scenarios.** For each: what is authored, what emerges, laws, data, cross-domain connection, special-case rule needed?

1. Steel pipe falls across energised conductors: authored, the pipe, the line, the supply; emerges, contact → a conducting path (the pipe is steel, C-layer conductivity) → current by the circuit solve → breaker trip or heating of the pipe (F-2.5) → sparks (observation). Laws F-3 (contact), F-2.2/2.3 (circuit), F-2.5. Data: conductivities, breaker curve. Cross-domain: mechanical contact becomes an electrical node. No special rule: contact already creates a shared node for heat; it must also create one for charge (a missing coupling, not a rule).
2. Floodwater reaches electrical equipment: authored, the water level input; emerges, immersion → a resistive path through water (data) → leakage current, trips, heating, a flat battery. Cross-domain: water level → circuit topology. No special rule once immersion is a contact with a conducting fluid.
3. A wooden beam burns until it cannot carry the roof: authored, the fire's ignition; emerges only once combustion exists (not yet modelled): char rate (data) reduces the section → capacity falls (C-layer) → F-4 yield → collapse. Cross-domain: thermal → section → structure. No special rule; a missing constitutive model (combustion) is the gap.
4. A vehicle crashes into a gas line: emerges: impact → line yields (F-4) → leak (a flow out of a store, by pressure and orifice: hydraulics-class model, not yet present) → gas as a species (combustion gap) → nothing further until combustion exists. No special rule; two missing models.
5. Battery robot falls into seawater: emerges: buoyancy/drag (exists), water as a conductor across the terminals (as 2), corrosion (not modelled; a slow state), the servos stalled by drag (F-2.3.1). No special rule.
6. A shaft loses a bearing: emerges: the bearing's rows vanish from the mechanism (a connection broken by F-4 at its capacity) → the shaft is held by what remains → misalignment, contact with the housing, friction heat, gear mesh lost. Higher-level consequences (machine unavailable) are reads of the state by the semantic layer, not scripts. No special rule.
7. A magnet heats until its properties change: exists in part: Br falls with temperature (data: temperature coefficient; demagnetisation at the Curie point as a state threshold in the C-layer); heat from eddy currents (M4) and conduction. No special rule.
8. A servo stalls until its winding overheats: emerges once the servo is a motor (F-2.3.1): stall current i = T_s/(N k), heat i²R, winding temperature by m c and cooling, resistance rising with temperature (copper α), torque falling, burnout at the insulation limit (data). Today: does not emerge (the servo has no winding). No special rule; the derivation is the fix.
9. Wind tears roofing off and it strikes another structure: emerges: wind load (data) over fastener capacity (NDS) → the sheet becomes a free body → carried by drag → impact on the next structure (contacts, F-4). Cross-domain: field → structure → body → structure. No special rule.
10. A machine vibrates itself loose over time: emerges only partly: bolt preload exists (clamp joints), loosening needs a slip-based model of self-loosening (Junker; a constitutive law with a history state) not present; the vibration itself exists (within rigid segments). No special rule; a missing model.
11. A creature loses a limb and adapts its gait: emerges with the limb ganglion (F-6 load feedback): the remaining legs' phases re-time because their load pattern changed; today, with fixed phases, it does not. No special rule; the design is the fix.
12. A bridge oscillates under wind and traffic: emerges within the segment model (discrete modes), with damping from the plastic hinges' dissipation; flutter needs flexible aerodynamics (not present). No special rule; a model limit, declared.
13. Fire changes air temperature and airflow, which changes the fire: needs combustion and a fluid field (two missing structures); with them, it emerges (buoyant flow is just density differences in the field). No special rule.
14. A boat takes on water and its stability changes continuously: emerges: water as added mass inside the hull volume (a flow into a store by the opening and pressure head) → the centre of mass and the immersed volume change → righting moment changes (hydrostatics exists at the face-area level) → capsize. No special rule; the flooding model is a hydraulics-class store.
15. A spacecraft expels mass and changes momentum: emerges from F-1 with a momentum port for the exhaust: the craft's momentum change equals the expelled mass times its relative velocity, booked. No special rule; the environment model (central gravity, no ground) is a declared change of M-3.

No scenario needs a special-case rule. Six need models that do not exist (combustion, a fluid field, self-loosening, corrosion, flooding stores, gas leaks), and all six are constitutive or structural additions under the same laws.

## 10. How the architecture stops an impossible state

| Proposal | Where it is stopped | By what |
|---|---|---|
| infinite-strength material | the C-layer: a material is a sourced record; an edit without a source is refused; an estimate is labelled and bounded by analogy | A-5, ML-6 |
| massless member carrying load | the inertia derivation: mass comes from volume × density of a real material; zero density has no source | A-5; also R-1 divides by mass |
| free energy | ML-3: every element is store, convex dissipator, Dirac junction or declared source with a finite store; an element that is none is not placeable | A-1, A-3 |
| motor torque without current | F-2.3: τ = k i, with i from the circuit solve; there is no other torque input | A-1 |
| teleportation | the only writer of poses is the tick, through rows; an edit of a pose in the build phase is a new initial state, not a transition, and must be admissible (no overlap) | ML-4 |
| controller reading hidden variables | ML-7: a controller's input type is its sensor readings; the mind cannot be handed a pose (today it can; §7 item 19) | A-1 scope |
| impossible heat removal | F-2.5: cooling is conduction and radiation by data; a heat sink is a store with a capacity; no "set temperature" | A-1, A-3 |
| overlapping solids | M-4 in A; placement snaps or refuses; the tick's contacts push apart within the slop | ML-4 |
| impossible acceleration | a = F/m with F bounded by every actuator's and contact's set (F-4) and every source's power (ML-3) | derived |
| arbitrary battery capacity | a pack is cells of a chemistry with sourced capacity at rate; the number is not an input | A-5 |
| impossible manufacturing process | R11 and the process table: a feature is made by a process that works that material and shape, or it is refused | A-5, ML-6 |

The boundary: the AI proposes a D' (and controller designs). The kernel computes whether D' ∈ A (geometry, realisability, constitutive range) and whether the implied elements satisfy ML-3 (structure) and ML-7 (information). If yes, D' is written and the tick takes over. If not, the refusal names the node. Nothing in the tick consults the AI, and nothing the AI wrote survives the gate unexamined. That is the validation boundary.

## 12. Trying to break the system: composition attacks found

1. **Two servos through a stiff link (energy loop).** A and B are each a passive link; each servo is a bounded source; connected, their two loops can fight through the link and pump energy at the loop frequency if the link's stiffness puts that frequency near their loops' bandwidth. The parent law is complete (ML-3: the sources are declared and bounded), but the realisation's obligation is incomplete: the soft servo row's damping was derived for one servo on one inertia (servoLoop), not for two coupled. Repair at the parent: the servo's loop (k, c) must be derived from the motor and controller (F-2.3.1), after which the pair's stability is a property of real gains, not of a chosen 6 Hz; and the per-tick ΔH ≤ port work assertion (P-9) makes any pumping visible with the rows that did it.
2. **Momentum leak through closure (position level).** A (velocity solve) is exact; B (closure) is a valid projection; composed, the centre of mass drifts (§6.4). Parent incomplete: F-1 was stated at velocity level only. Repaired in §3 by F-1.1.2.
3. **Latch alternating with the continuous model (solver conflict).** A (continuous magnet force) and B (latch constraint) are each passive; alternating them each tick near the release threshold could add energy if the release hands back more than the contact could take. M6 handles this by construction (the hand-back is the inadmissible part only) and the tests check agreement with the continuous model; the obligation to prove, not yet proved, is that the alternation is passive over any sequence; P-9 would catch it.
4. **Hidden information channel: the gait op.** The herd writes servo amplitudes; the world reads them next tick. Valid both ways. But the mind receives `you` from the world, so the loop controller → world → controller carries information no sensor gave: a channel outside ML-7. Parent complete; the realisation (mind's inputs) violates it.
5. **Time-scale aliasing: magnets at 16 substeps and a servo at the tick.** Each is resolved on its own; a servo on a magnet latch sees the latch's constraint change once a tick while the field changes 16 times. ML-5 covers it (the slower element sees an averaged fast one, r-RESPA), but the servo's loop can alias the latch's release. Repair: the latch's state change must be an event the slower loop sees at its own rate (already true: once a tick). Declared, not broken.
6. **Double-counted dissipation.** Jolt applies friction on bodies outside the re-solve; the re-solve applies friction on contacts it recorded; A15 routes each contact to exactly one of them. If a body enters the watched set mid-tick (a part newly bonded), a contact could be counted by both for one tick. Obligation: the routing is decided before the step; the test is P-9 per tick (a double count shows as negative `numerical`).
7. **Double-counted energy storage: the rotor.** With the rotor row (saved patch) the rotor's kinetic energy ½J ω_rel² is a store the ledger does not list; its work would show as `numerical` until booked. Found by the ledger's design, before it is applied.
8. **Conflicting ownership of state: servo aim.** The world owns `c.aim` (from rhythm and channel); the herd owns amplitude; registry owns offset. Three writers of one target. Valid separately; composed, a change of `swing` in the registry and a gait amplitude in the herd can exceed the travel between them if either forgets the other. Repair at the parent: F-3.4 says travel is in A and commands are clamped by the gate, not by their writers.
9. **Recursive controller loop: Ego and the watchdog.** The watchdog reports jitter; Ego stills the part; the stilled walker falls; the fall is reported. Each step valid. The loop existed (R14's origin). Parent repair: the watchdog reports obligations, never actions, and Ego acts only through proposals to the gate; a mind's drive is a declared source and cannot be "stilled" by a diagnostic.
10. **Save/load with undeclared state.** D round-trips; X partly does not (charge, warm start, seeds, latches). Composition of "save" and "load" is a valid pair whose result is a different future. Parent incomplete: A-4's identity policy must list X in full; repair is to declare and save it, or declare it as re-derived with a bound.

## 13. Checklists

**Before implementing any physical feature:**
1. What is the thing: store, dissipator, source, transformer, gyrator, junction, constraint, sensor or controller? If none, it is not an element and cannot enter.
2. What state does it add to X? Is every member saved (I2) and finite (A-4)?
3. What energy does it store, as what function of its state? What does it dissipate, by what convex potential? What power ports does it have, with what effort and flow?
4. What momentum does it exchange, and across which cut? Is the exchange written as a pair about one line?
5. What constraints does it add to A? Which are bilateral, which unilateral? What are its hardware limits, as geometry?
6. What constitutive relations does it need, and what is the source of every number? Which numbers are estimates, and what are they derived from?
7. What symmetry must hold? Is every expression in relative or intrinsic variables? Is every threshold dimensionless or scaled by a declared quantity?
8. What may its controller know? Which sensors, with what model and latency?
9. What are its time scales and stiffnesses? Which modes need substeps or implicit integration (ML-5)? What is the resolution length for transport?
10. What dimensionless groups govern it, and at what values does the model stop holding?
11. Which existing laws does it instantiate (F-1..F-6), and which realisations will carry it (R-1..R-11)? Does any realisation gain a new obligation?
12. What proof obligations does it introduce, and what property test over random inputs falsifies each?
13. What does it cost per tick, and how is the cost bounded?
14. What does it change in D's schema, and does the codec round-trip it?
15. What legacy routine, if any, now duplicates its behaviour, and when is that routine removed?

**After implementing:**
law compliance (every new line names its node); invariant checks (P-1..P-20 instantiated); energy accounting (the ledger closes with the new terms, `numerical` unchanged); momentum accounting (internal pairs sum to zero); constraint checks (residual bounded); frame tests (rotated scene); scale tests (λ-scaled scene at matched groups); composition tests (joined to a random passive partner); boundary tests (at each dimensionless limit); adversarial tests (random warm starts, random orders, extreme mass ratios, zero gravity, a single body, an empty scene); floating-point sensitivity (summation order); timestep sensitivity (dt/2); iteration sensitivity (passes × 2); save/load determinism; failure-mode checks (over capacity → the declared failed state and nothing else); cross-domain checks (its heat, its current, its loads reach the ledgers they should); property-based randomised tests as the default form; the budget; the whole suite green.

## 14. Failure, constants, emergence

**How physical failure enters the hierarchy.** Never as "if force > X, break". A failure is a point where a constitutive response leaves one regime for another, decided by the laws acting on data and history:

| Phenomenon | Level | How it enters |
|---|---|---|
| yield | F-4 (an admissible set K with |σ| ≤ σ_y) | plastic flow is maximum dissipation at the surface; exists for hinges and clamp slip |
| plastic deformation | history state in X (plastic rotation) | exists for breakable parts |
| fracture | state transition when accumulated plastic work or rotation reaches a datum | exists |
| fatigue | history state (accumulated damage, Miner) with S-N data | not implemented |
| thermal damage | constitutive dependence on temperature (strength, resistance, Br) with limits (burnout, Curie, Draper glow) | partly (resistance, glow); burnout exists for motors |
| electrical damage | winding temperature over insulation class; breaker curves | motors yes; breakers no |
| wear | history state with a wear law (Archard) | not implemented |
| creep | history state, rate law | not implemented |
| buckling | a stability limit derived from geometry and E (Euler/Johnson in the ganglia); in the world it would emerge only with flexible members | ganglia yes; world no (M-2) |
| loss of magnetism | temperature coefficient and Curie point (C-layer) | partly |
| battery degradation | slow state with cycle data | not implemented |

| Quantity | Class |
|---|---|

**What should be emergent, and from what.**
walking, running, animal locomotion: from servos or muscles (F-2.3) on links under friction (F-4) and gravity, with a controller that only sets phases and gains; the gait and its transition speed come from Froude (F-5). falling: from gravity and the support polygon (F-3). slipping: from the friction cone (F-4). breaking: from load over capacity (F-4 surface + state). overheating: from dissipation booked to parts (F-2.5) with cooling data. battery depletion: from charge integrated from current (F-2.2). machine overload: from current limits and torque–speed lines (F-2.3). structural collapse: from member failure propagating through the load path (F-1.2, F-4). floating, sinking: from buoyancy versus weight (hydrostatics, C). stalling: from torque–speed and load (F-2.3). vibration: from stiffness and mass (within M-2's segment limit). sound: from impact and friction power (observation of the ledger). fire spread: needs combustion (missing), then from heat flux and ignition data. flooding: from runoff and drain capacity (hydraulics class, missing in the world). traffic congestion: from vehicles with controllers that see each other (F-6) on roads; emergent once vehicles and minds exist.
